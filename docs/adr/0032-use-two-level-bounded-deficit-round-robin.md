# Use two-level bounded deficit round robin

Nereus Delay V1 uses one exact fairness family: weighted deficit round robin (DRR) first across `ACTIVE_FOR_COMMANDS` shards that have at least one Lane with `admissionGate=OPEN && runtimeReadiness=READY` and then across only those Lanes inside the selected shard. Priority queues are used only to discover eligibility time; they do not repeatedly choose the lowest timestamp and starve other tenants or targets.

## Ready index

Each `OPEN + READY` Lane with schedulable work has one versioned persistent ready entry keyed by `nextEligibleAt`, and Lane state stores the optional exact current key; every other Lane has none. For an unordered Lane this is its earliest due/retry record. For `DELIVERY_TIME_FIFO`, the `ORDERED` key first selects the blocking head by business `(deliverAt, effective Schedule Source Position, delayMessageId)`, then that head contributes `headEligibilityAt=max(actionAt,retryEligibilityAt)` to `nextEligibleAt`; certified Pulsar handoff's fixed Profile-version lead preserves this order. Cancel, Reschedule, retry, circuit change, Claim, terminal, Pause/Resume, Break/Close, readiness, and owner change atomically delete the old key, increment runtime `laneVersion`, and optionally insert the replacement. This index revision is distinct from the source-ordered `laneControlVersion`. Versioning rejects an old key visible to a concurrent snapshot; unexplained persistent stale/missing/gate-inconsistent keys fence scheduling and require deterministic rebuild.

The scheduler arms a timer for the earliest ready key but also has a bounded wake interval for recovery and drift. Due discovery never restarts from the smallest key on every cycle: each shard persists a rotating `readyDiscoveryCursor`, promotes each due Lane once into a bounded active DRR ring, and continues from the successor after key/byte/time or permit exhaustion. Reaching the end wraps to the first due key and increments a round. A continuously hot early key already in the ring cannot consume the discovery prefix again and hide another due key. Owner activation performs a bounded complete pass over the ready index (bounded by the Lane grant) before publishing, then persists cursor/ring generation and rejects duplicate/stale entries.

Expiry is not a READY eligibility reason. A separate versioned `timeline_cf/EXPIRY` index remains present for paused/blocked/recovering/broken Lanes and wakes at `expireAt` to enqueue a logged expiration mutation. Circuit or retry deadlines cannot hide an earlier expiry.

## DRR visits

- Each Worker shard and each Lane has a positive integer weight and byte quantum. On one visit, its deficit increases by `weight * baseQuantumBytes` up to a configured cap.
- Record cost is `max(accountedPublishBytes, minimumRecordCost)`. A Lane may Claim while cost fits its deficit and all persistent/in-memory message/byte/rate permits, subject to per-visit message, byte, and elapsed-time caps.
- Deficit remains for a ready Lane across rounds and is reset or bounded when it becomes empty/ineligible. The cap must be at least the maximum admitted record cost, so a valid large message cannot starve forever.
- Ordered Lanes admit only their single head. Unordered Lanes may admit a bounded group. Adapter batching may combine only already-admitted compatible records from that same Lane and never changes individual durable outcomes.
- Lack of Worker/global permits ends the admission cycle without moving additional records to inflight. Failed Lane/circuit eligibility is moved to its persisted next probe time.

Each shard DB persists its inner Lane cursor, active-ring generation, capped deficits, round and `lastServedRound` in the five closed `meta_cf/SCHEDULER` values at each bounded cycle boundary (and with a successful Claim). Permit exhaustion resumes at the successor, never at ring head. At most one uncheckpointed visit may repeat after crash. On owner/store recovery the initial rotation uses retained `lastServedRound` plus the new Owner identity, and the first round cannot serve one Lane twice before every discovered eligible Lane once; repeated-restart tests enforce this rule. The Worker-level outer shard DRR is bounded process state reconstructed from the finite open `ACTIVE_FOR_COMMANDS` DB set and offers every eligible shard once before repeating one; there is no fictitious cross-DB atomic Worker-ring record. The progress guarantee itself applies while an Owner remains `ACTIVE_FOR_COMMANDS`.

No “first oversized record” exception bypasses caps. Route/Profile validation and startup require `maxBytesPerVisit`, every Lane/shard/Worker inflight-byte cap, Adapter physical-byte cap, deficit cap, and the non-reserved Worker share each to accommodate the maximum admitted Prepared Publish. Message caps are at least one. A record that fits the public payload/profile limit therefore eventually fits every scheduler dimension; otherwise the Profile/Worker cannot activate.

Given a bounded number of ready shards/Lanes, nonzero weight, an admitted record within every certified cap, an `ACTIVE_FOR_COMMANDS` owner, an `OPEN + READY` Lane, and recurring reserved global capacity, every healthy schedulable Lane is visited once per complete round and receives enough deficit within `ceil(recordCost / (weight * baseQuantumBytes))` capped rounds. The capacity artifact derives `maxRoundDuration` from the maximum discovered shards/Lanes, visit-time caps, event-loop scheduler share, and handoff overhead, then derives `maxHealthyLaneServiceGap`. Runtime publishes observed round duration, discovery age, and per-Lane service gap against those exact thresholds.

## Work-class isolation

Shard Log application, ownership/lease events, system-mutation/outcome production, query reads, GC, checkpoint work, expiry discovery, and due scheduling have separate bounded queues/pools. Every class has a mandatory positive weight, queue record/byte caps, per-turn record/byte/elapsed caps and `maxEventLoopClassDelay`; correctness/outcome, expiry, flush and capacity-releasing GC minima are non-borrowable. Lease loss can close the gate immediately. Borrowed tokens are reacquired at each bounded chunk and reclaim within `maxBorrowedResourceHoldTime`. A source turn cannot exceed its caps, and a due burst cannot postpone source or outcome-event work beyond the configured bound. These limits, not thread separation alone, prevent a Command flood from starving callbacks/expiry or a due burst from starving source application.

Claim first acquires ephemeral executor permits and then atomically persists exact permit accounting. Write failure releases them. Claim has a bounded materialization deadline and remains reversible; timeout returns it to its prior timeline state without consuming a Publish Attempt. On recovery, executor permits are rebuilt from exact inflight state before scheduling begins.

## Acceptance

Deterministic virtual-time tests cover unequal weights, one maximum-size record, continuously replenished hot early-index Lanes, rotating discovery wrap, permit exhaustion at every cursor, repeated owner restart, ordered head retry, open circuits, Worker-wide shard imbalance, Cancel during Claim, stale ready keys, clock-gate pauses, source floods, and callback floods. No healthy continuously ready Lane may exceed the configured/proven discovery/round/service-gap bound without a typed lack-of-capacity or non-healthy interval in evidence.
