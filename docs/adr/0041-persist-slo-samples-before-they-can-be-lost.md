# Persist SLO samples before they can be lost

Nereus Delay does not treat a best-effort metric scrape as proof of an SLO denominator. Every published production or release objective uses the Protocol Registry's immutable objective digest, closed sample-event identity, domain-separated sample ID, durable Start, and monotonically merged Final evidence. A restart, timeout, uncertain result, clock failure, exporter retry, or rolling-window transition cannot silently remove an accepted sample or turn a previously bad observation into a good one.

## Decision

- A Start must be durable before the measured component can lose ownership of the operation, or be exactly reconstructible from an already durable authority. Shard-derived starts use Source Positions, Message/Attempt/Lane/Recovery records and materialize through `meta_cf/SLO_OUTBOX`. SDK enqueue/native paths sync their own bounded outbox before Producer ownership; Gateway and control/RTO detectors do the same before processing the measured request/cut. A component without this facility is excluded from claiming the production objective, not from the denominator after the fact.
- The success event remains the exact semantic event in the main design. For Admission/application it is the authoritative RocksDB WAL sync; for queued/native operations it is the guarded Broker durability receipt. A Final observation may be written only after that event. In particular, the enqueue-time `PUBLISH_ADMISSION.decision_time` is qualification evidence and cannot stand in for the later Admission-WAL SLO endpoint.
- Final evidence is durably outboxed before export. If a process dies after the semantic success but before Final sync, recovery recreates the same sample from its authority and records a later conservative endpoint or a closed bad-time outcome. An open sample past its deadline is `BAD_TIMEOUT`; uncertainty, unqualified time, and evidence gaps are explicit bad outcomes.
- Export is at-least-once. The collector keys by `(sampleId,startDigest)` and applies the Registry's monotonic merge: a bad result never becomes success, `AT_MOST` keeps the worse maximum, `AT_LEAST` keeps the worse minimum, and exclusion/gap evidence is never erased. Same sample ID with different Start bytes is integrity failure.
- Every `HEALTHY` due objective has the same-event `ALL_ACCEPTED` companion. ALL_ACCEPTED Start is materialized immediately from durable Message/eligibility authority. HEALTHY Start/Final is materialized from that same authority only after durable evidence proves the complete interval healthy; it is never tentatively created and deleted. Excluded records stay in the all-accepted denominator with exactly one closed reason. Capacity exhaustion or missing health/exclusion evidence produces `BAD_EVIDENCE_GAP` instead of shrinking either denominator.
- SLO state is observational. It neither enters the command-derived semantic digest nor authorizes, rejects, rolls back, or delays a Command/Admission/Producer side effect. Its record/byte/export-rate budget is bounded and disjoint from correctness and Outcome Reserve; release certification proves that budget for the declared load envelope.
- Collector raw evidence and merge history survive the full rolling window plus late-finalization, checkpoint replay and audit margin. Published reports bind objective, load-envelope, source and binary digests.

## Consequences

SDKs, Gateways, Workers and control-plane monitors need a small durable outbox or an equivalent synchronously durable evidence service. Final observation can conservatively overstate latency after a crash, which is intentional: availability evidence may become worse, but never falsely better. SLO instrumentation remains outside the business state machine, so an observability outage cannot create a target-dependent Command result or weaken delivery correctness.
